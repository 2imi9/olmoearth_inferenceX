

Short answer: with those 300 labels, **no part of this map can be certified** as wrong at most 5% of the time (exact test, alpha = 0.05, delta = 0.1, prefix rule).

| Zone (most-confident share) | Windows | Labels inside | Wrong | Upper bound @ delta=0.1 | Certified? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15% of map | 2,372 | 46 | 3 | 13.9% | ✗ |
| 25% of map | 3,953 | 68 | 3 | 9.5% | ✗ |
| 50% of map | 7,906 | 140 | 13 | 13.2% | ✗ |

Details:

- Population is 15,813 windows; a zone needs at least 45 labelled windows to be testable, so the smallest zone checked was 15% of the map, and all 18 levels tested failed.
- The closest case is the most-confident 25% zone (3 wrong of 68): its point estimate looks fine, but the exact upper bound is still 9.5%, above the 5% bar.
- For context, the full-map upper bound is 26.4% (69 wrong of 300).

What would change the answer:

- More simple-random labels overall (the design already meets the `random` requirement); you need enough labels concentrated in the high-confidence region with few errors that its exact upper bound drops below 5%.
- Note the guarantee applies to an alpha fixed **before** seeing the labels, so relaxing alpha afterwards is not a valid way to "earn" a certification.
